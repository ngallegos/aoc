Counting instead of building

Written lesson

Some problems describe something that grows step by step, then ask only how big it gets. You can often answer that without ever creating the thing.

The idea

Replace every letter at once: A becomes B, and B becomes AB. Starting from A you get B, AB, BAB, ABBAB. The string soon becomes too large to store.

Length does not depend on order, so keep only how many of each letter there are. With 2 As and 3 Bs, the next step has 3 As and 5 Bs: every A adds one B, and every B adds one of each.

When is this allowed?

Each part must change independently, without looking at its neighbors. If neighbors affect each other, look for bigger chunks that never do, and count those instead.

If working out what a part becomes is slow, do it the first time you see that part and save the result. This is called memoization.
